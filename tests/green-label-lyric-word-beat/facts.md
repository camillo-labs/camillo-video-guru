All facts come only from /Users/student/Documents/Claude/deck-research/03-green-label.md (prepared 18 September 2026, file dated 19 September). The line numbers refer to that file. Retrieved and checked on 12 October 2026. The quoted wording is the file's own.

## Facts cleared for captions

1. EU destruction share
   - Exact wording, line 22: "Unsold and returned textiles destroyed before use: 4 to 9% of all textile products placed on the EU market, equal to 264,000 to 594,000 tonnes a year."
   - Original source cited in the file: European Environment Agency briefing "The destruction of returned and unsold textiles in Europe's circular economy", 4 March 2024, https://www.eea.europa.eu/publications/the-destruction-of-returned-and
   - Suggested caption: "4 to 9% of textiles placed on the EU market are destroyed before use. EEA, 2024"
   - Caveat to keep in the credit or description (file, line 24, quoting the EEA authors): the overall size of textile product destruction "remains uncertain". The file also notes there is no UK-specific figure (line 10).
2. EU ban on destroying unsold clothes
   - Exact wording, line 9: "The EU ban on destroying unsold clothing, accessories and footwear is already law and already in application for large companies (since 19 July 2026). It does not cover fabric, and it does not cover the UK domestic market."
   - Line 163: "Dates: large enterprises from 19 July 2026 (already in application as of the date of this note). Medium-sized enterprises (50 to 249 employees, turnover up to €50 million) from 19 July 2030. Micro and small enterprises are exempt."
   - Sources cited in the file: Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, Article 25, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1781/oj and Commission news of 17 July 2026, https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/ban-destruction-unsold-clothes-and-shoes-enters-application-2026-07-17_en
   - Suggested caption: "Since 19 July 2026 the EU bans large companies from destroying unsold clothes." Say "large companies", because the ban is phased and does not cover fabric.
3. Tonnage
   - Exact wording, line 22: "264,000 to 594,000 tonnes a year." Same source as fact 1.
   - Suggested caption: "Up to 594,000 tonnes a year" only together with the 4 to 9% figure and the EEA credit.
4. EU textile waste
   - Exact wording, line 19: "Total textile waste generated in the EU-27 in 2022: 6.94 million tonnes, about 16 kg per person, stable since 2016."
   - Sources cited in the file: EEA briefing of 26 March 2025, https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/publications/circularity-of-the-eu-textiles-value-chain-in-numbers and ETC CE Report 2025/7.
   - Suggested caption: "6.94 million tonnes of textile waste in the EU in 2022. EEA"
5. UK residual textile waste (optional, not deadstock)
   - Exact wording, line 32: "711,000 tonnes (49% of used textiles, or 35 items per person a year) went into residual waste, of which 84% was incinerated with energy recovery and 11% landfilled."
   - Source cited in the file: WRAP, Textiles Market Situation Report 2024, published 1 May 2024, https://www.circularonline.co.uk/news/49-of-used-textiles-end-up-in-residual-waste-wrap-report-finds/
   - Use only if the caption says these are used textiles in the UK in 2022, not deadstock.

## Not to use

- UK deadstock or unsold-stock tonnage: the file says "NOT FOUND" (line 37). An estimate of 57,000 to 128,000 tonnes is built on an unverified assumption, so do not caption it.
- "92 million tonnes", "15% on the cutting room floor", "$120bn of unused fabric" and "30% of clothes never sold": the file marks them untraceable or without a primary source (lines 13 and 47 to 50).
- "5.6 million tonnes CO2 from destroyed textiles": the file calls quoting only the top of the range misleading (line 51).
- Any claim that the EU ban covers fabric or UK-only stock: the file says it does not (line 9 and line 168).
