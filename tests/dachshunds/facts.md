# Dachshund caption facts and their sources

Checked on 14 October 2026 against English Wikipedia "Dachshund" (revision 1376053316).

1. "Dachshund means badger dog": Etymology section, "The name Dachshund is of German origin, and means 'badger dog', from Dachs ('badger') and Hund ('dog, hound')."
2. "Bred to flush out badgers": lead, "It was bred to scent, chase, and flush out badgers and other burrow-dwelling animals."
3. "Smooth, wire or long haired": lead, "It may be smooth-haired, wire-haired, or long-haired, in various colors."
4. "A German breed": lead, "a German breed of short-legged, long-bodied dog of hound type."

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dachshund

## HUD cut (hud/out/edit.mp4, hud-lyric-heat), checked 14 October 2026

Pages opened: English Wikipedia "Dachshund" (rev 1376053316), "Waldi" (rev 1365902340), "1972 Summer Olympics" (rev 1377029188) and "List of Olympic mascots" (rev 1379242219). The Olympics site (olympics.com) did not answer from this machine, so the Olympic fact rests on Wikipedia, which cites it.

1. BADGER HUNTER: Dachshund, "It was bred to scent, chase, and flush out badgers and other burrow-dwelling animals." and "originally used as badger hunters".
2. FIRST OLYMPIC MASCOT: Waldi, "Waldi ... was the first official Olympic mascot, created for the 1972 Summer Olympics in Munich." Dachshund page: "chosen as the first official mascot for the 1972 Summer Olympics in Munich, with the name Waldi."
3. BORN TO TUNNEL: Dachshund, "Its skin is loose enough not to tear while tunneling in tight burrows" and the curved tail "to help haul the dog out if it becomes stuck in a burrow". Playful paraphrase of those lines.
4. LOUD BARK: Dachshund, "They can have a loud bark." The page does not say the bark was bred for the job, so no such claim is made.
5. WIENER OR SAUSAGE: Dachshund, "nicknamed the wiener dog in the US and Canada or the sausage dog in the British Isles and Australasia."
6. 300 YEARS OF ATTITUDE: Dachshund, first verifiable references "came from books written in the early 18th century" (about 300 years). Sources disagree, the AKC says the 15th century. "Attitude" is a joke on "they will challenge a larger dog". It is in the plan (edl.json) but was not in the frames I sampled.
